# DS claims

These rules come from the DS editing guide in ai-writing-suite (notes/ai-editing-guide-for-ds.md). They keep an analysis doc from sounding more certain than its evidence.

## Every claim points at its evidence

A number or causal claim names where it comes from: the table, the chart, the check output or the query. If the evidence isn't in the doc or its appendix, the claim isn't either.

## Hedges match the evidence

Judge each claim as under-claimed, well calibrated or over-claimed.

- The answer carries no hedge. If the answer needs one, the answer is "can't tell yet" plus what would settle it.
- Elsewhere, use about one hedge per claim, and only where the evidence is weaker than the sentence.
- "Proves", "definitively" and "clearly" are over-claims unless a designed test backs them.

## Statistical words need a design

"Statistically significant", "causal", "caused", "drives" and "driven by" need the test or design named in the same paragraph: a randomized experiment, a holdout, an interval, a p-value. A before-and-after comparison supports "coincided with", not "caused".

## The "so what" comes from the evidence

The implication follows from what the analysis measured, and its label says how far it goes:

- measured: computed from the data.
- inferred: follows from measured facts by reasoning.
- guess: neither.

Never write a recommendation the evidence section doesn't support.

## Numbers read one way

- Give a rate change in percentage points (pp) and a relative change in percent, and say which one you mean.
- Give the period, the denominator and the sample size once, where the number first appears.
- Use the same precision for numbers the reader compares.
- Report the number, then its uncertainty: "-1.8 pp (95% CI -2.3 to -1.3)".

## Voice

No first person in findings ("CTR fell", not "I found CTR fell"). First person is fine for a recommendation that is your judgment.
