ds-stack

A pstack-style plugin for data-science work. It doesn't make an agent faster. It makes the agent's number something you can act on: scripts check the mechanics, the agent rules alternative explanations in or out with evidence, and every answer ends in the same readable readout.

The checks

Four scripts, one evidence rule

Each check takes aggregates as JSON and returns pass, flag, fail or could-not-run. A flag is a finding the answer must report, such as a Simpson's paradox; a fail is a data or query defect. A check counts as evidenced only with at least two real incidents behind it; the rest stay provisional until the wrong-number archive backs them or they get removed.

What it shows. Every setup got all five cases right, and plain prompting missed none, so these cases are too easy to separate the setups. Your own calibration rule wants the baseline to miss 30–40%. The harness does work: checks ran and were logged in every checks-only and full run, and no run was contaminated or unreadable. The full setup costs about 40% more per run.

A bug the eval caught. In round 1, the full setup said "can't tell" on the join case in 2 of 3 runs. A rule in the skill said to abstain whenever a check couldn't run, and with only one week of data, completeness had nothing to compare against, so the agent held back a correct 55.0%. The rule now separates a failed check, a check called wrongly, and data that can't support a check. The table shows round 2. Because round 2 reran the same cases, it confirms the fix but doesn't prove it generalizes.

This is a smoke test of the harness, not a verdict on v0. The pre-registered rule needs at least 15 held-out cases and refuses to call a pass below that. The cases are synthetic, and every setup also got the readout format, which may be doing much of the work on its own. The real test is Stage 5 with your own incidents, then Stage 6 on real work.

What the research changed

Across benchmarks and vendor guidance, the largest measured lever is curated, data-verified metric definitions (+26 to +36 points on hard DABStep tasks). Execute-and-verify loops add 2 to 6 points, and no mainstream benchmark scores abstention. So ds-mode now pins the metric from metrics.json before querying, asks when two readings give different numbers, ends with provenance, and allows "caused" only with a design that identifies the cause.

Still open: an independent reviewer before a readout is sent, answerability cases in the eval, and an eval built from your real past errors that crosses definitions in context with checks on and off.

What happens on your machines

  1. Stage 0. On the work laptop, create the overlay outside any repo, fill in the denylist, and enable the hook. Decide adopt or retire for each of your existing DS tools.
  2. Stage 1. Write the wrong-number archive: at least 10 incidents, kept on the machine where they happened. Write and verify metrics.json entries for the metrics you report most; don't let an agent draft them.
  3. Stage 2. Replace the draft method in SKILL.md with your own first five moves on a metric drop.
  4. Stage 3. Back each provisional check with real incidents, or delete it.
  5. Stages 5–6. Build at least 15 held-out cases from your incidents, run the three setups, then use v0 on real work for 2–4 weeks, logging catches, false alarms and minutes.
