No, it doesn't change the plan; it's the reason the plan defines 3P users at the user level. A '3P power' tenant is a sales label based on how many connectors are installed, so many of its users may never touch third-party content. If I used the tenant label as the segment, those users would dilute the 3P estimate with people the panel can't plausibly help through 3P search. The reverse also holds: 'standard' tenants can contain heavy 3P searchers, who would be missed. So the segment comes from each user's pre-test `used_3p_result` share. The tenant label stays as a secondary heterogeneity cut, and the plan already says to report how well it overlaps with the user-level definition. The overlap check now matters more: I'd report the share of users in '3P power' tenants who are 3P users, and the share of 3P users who sit in 'standard' tenants. The closing lines are unchanged.

Unit: user (the randomization unit), with per-user success rate over the test period as the outcome
Segment: a user is a 3P user if their pre-test sessions (2026-06-01 to 2026-08-31) show a high share with used_3p_result (threshold set before looking at outcomes); not the sales tenant label, and not test-period 3P usage
Tenants: as randomization blocks (tenant fixed effects, standard errors clustered by tenant), reported both user-weighted and tenant-weighted, with per-tenant effects checked for domination by large tenants
